Storage

How to Store Trading Cards in Singapore's Humidity

By Cardboard Clinic · 3 min read

Most of the warped and curled cards we see in Singapore were never dropped or bent. They were simply stored in the wrong place. Our weather is warm and humid all year, and paper soaks up moisture from the air. Here is how to keep your collection flat, clean and safe.

Why humidity is the enemy

Paper takes in moisture when the air is damp and gives it back when the air is dry. As it does, it swells and shrinks. A trading card is made of several layers that do not all react at the same speed, so when humidity changes, one side of the card moves more than the other and the card curls.

  • Holo and foil cards curl most. The foil layer barely moves while the paper behind it swells, so foil cards often bow toward the front.
  • Fast swings are worse than steady damp. Moving cards between an air-conditioned room and a humid one, again and again, is a common cause of curl.
  • Long-term damp can lead to musty smells, surface spotting and sleeves sticking to the card.

The basics: sleeves, toploaders and binders

  • Use a soft penny sleeve first, then a toploader or semi-rigid holder for anything you care about.
  • Choose acid-free, PVC-free sleeves and binder pages. Old PVC pages can leave residue on cards over time.
  • Do not overfill binder pockets or force cards into tight toploaders. That is how edge dents start.
  • Store toploaders and boxes upright, not under heavy stacks.

Control the air around your cards

In Singapore, the single most useful thing you can do for a valuable collection is to keep it in a dry, steady place.

  • A dry box or dry cabinet, the kind used for cameras, works very well. Many collectors keep it at a moderate setting, roughly 45 to 55% relative humidity. Do not go very dry, as that can make cards brittle and curl the other way.
  • A sealed box with silica gel packs is a cheap alternative. Use a small hygrometer to check the level, and dry out or replace the packs when they are saturated.
  • Keep cards away from windows, direct sun, the kitchen and bathroom, and the direct airflow of an air-conditioner.
  • When you take cards out of a dry box, let them settle in their sleeves before handling, rather than moving them back and forth all day.

Graded slabs are not immune

A slab protects against handling, but the card inside still responds to humidity over time, and condensation can form if a slab moves quickly from a cold room into warm air. Store slabs upright in a dry, steady place too.

If a card has already curled

A mild curl from humidity often relaxes on its own after a few days in a toploader in a steady, moderately dry place. Do not try to iron it, bend it back or put it in the fridge. Common questions

What humidity should trading cards be stored at?

Many collectors aim for a moderate, steady level, roughly 45 to 55% relative humidity. Very dry storage can make cards brittle, and very damp storage leads to curl and spotting. Steady matters as much as the number.

Why do my holo Pokémon cards curl?

The foil layer reacts to humidity differently from the paper behind it. When the paper swells in humid air and the foil does not, the card bows. Steady, moderately dry storage helps a lot.

Is a dry cabinet worth it for trading cards in Singapore?

For a valuable collection, yes. A camera-style dry box or cabinet keeps humidity steady, which prevents most curl and warping. A sealed box with silica gel and a hygrometer is a cheaper alternative.

Can a warped card be flattened?
