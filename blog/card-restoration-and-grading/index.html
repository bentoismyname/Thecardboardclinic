Card Restoration and Grading: What Collectors Should Know

By Cardboard Clinic · 3 min read

One of the first questions collectors ask us is: if you fix my card, will it grade higher? The honest answer is that nobody can promise that. Here is what restoration can and cannot do, how grading companies may see a treated card, and how to make a sensible decision.

Cardboard Clinic is an independent service. We are not affiliated with PSA, BGS, CGC or any card publisher, and grading companies make their own decisions.

Repair and grading are different goals

Repair is about the card itself: easing a dent, softening a crease, cleaning a surface, flattening a curl, so the card looks and sits right again. Grading is a third party's opinion of a card's condition and authenticity at a point in time. A card can look much better after treatment and still not receive the grade its owner hoped for.

How graders may view treated cards

Each grading company sets its own standards, and those standards can change. In general:

  • Cards that show evidence of alteration, such as trimming, recolouring, added substances or certain kinds of surface work, may be marked as altered, given a qualifier, or not graded at all.
  • Some treatments, such as pressing out a dent, are a debated grey area. Graders may or may not detect them, and may or may not treat them as alteration.
  • Surface cleaning of loose dust and contamination is generally seen differently from treatments that change the card's structure, but the final call is always the grader's.

Before submitting a treated card, read the grading company's current published policies, and be honest about any work done if they ask.

What we will and won't do

  • We do not trim, recolour, repaint or add fillers to cards.
  • We do not guarantee any grade, and we do not quote prices based on a hoped-for graded value. Our pricing uses the card's current ungraded value.
  • We do explain the possible grading implications for your specific card before any work, and every card needs your signed consent before treatment starts.
  • We do photograph and record the condition before and after, so you have an honest record of what was done.

Slab extraction

If a graded card needs treatment, it has to come out of its slab first. Removing a card from a slab carries a small risk and permanently ends that grade, so we only do it with the owner's clear consent and an understanding of the risk.

So, is restoration worth it before grading?

It can be, when your main goal is a card that looks and feels right, and any grade is a bonus. It is usually not worth it if the whole point is to flip the card for a higher grade, because no outcome is guaranteed and the card may be flagged.

A good way to decide:

  1. Ask yourself whether you would be happy with the card after treatment even if it never went into a slab.
  2. Check what the card is worth today, ungraded, not what it might be worth as a 10.
  3. Send us photos on WhatsApp. We will tell you honestly what treatment can do for that card and what it might mean for grading.

Common questions

Will restoring my card get it a higher PSA grade?

No grade can be guaranteed. Grading companies decide independently, and some treatments may lead a card to be marked as altered or not graded. Restoration is best when you want the card itself to look right.

Can grading companies detect card pressing?

Sometimes. Pressing is a grey area: it may or may not be detected, and graders may or may not treat it as alteration. Check the grading company's current policy before submitting a treated card.

Do you trim or recolour cards?

No. We do not trim, recolour, repaint or add fillers to cards.

Can you remove a card from a graded slab?

Yes, with the owner's consent. Removing a card from a slab carries a small risk and ends that grade permanently, so we explain this before doing it.
