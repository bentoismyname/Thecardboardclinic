Dents

Can a Dented Pokémon Card Be Fixed?

By Cardboard Clinic · 3 min read

A dent is one of the most common problems we see on Pokémon and other trading cards, and one of the most fixable. Here is what a dent actually is, why home fixes often make it worse, and what you can realistically expect from professional dent reduction in Singapore.

What a dent really is

A trading card is not a single sheet. It is several thin layers pressed together: a protective coating, the printed front, paper stock, a dark core layer and the printed back. When something presses into the card, such as a fingernail, the edge of a toploader or a heavy box, the paper fibres in those layers are squashed together. The ink and coating usually stay intact, but the surface now has a low spot that catches the light.

That is why a dent can look terrible under a lamp and almost disappear when you tilt the card. It is a change in shape, not missing material, and that is exactly why it can often be improved.

The common types

  • Surface indents: small pressure marks on the face, often from writing on something placed on top of the card.
  • Edge dings: a dent along an edge, usually from the card being knocked or pushed into a sleeve or toploader.
  • Corner dents: a corner pushed in or bent slightly, often with the layers starting to lift.
  • Roller or print lines: lines from the factory. These are not damage and usually cannot be changed.

Why home fixes are risky

You will find plenty of videos showing cards being steamed, put in the fridge, ironed or left under a pile of books. Some of them work on some cards. Many of them quietly cause new problems, especially in Singapore's humidity.

  • Water and steam can leave tide marks, cloud the surface coating or make the layers swell unevenly, which turns a small dent into a wavy card.
  • Heat can soften the coating and the holofoil. On holo and full-art cards that can mean a change in texture that is far more obvious than the original dent.
  • Heavy books spread pressure unevenly. The dent may soften, but the card can pick up a curl or new marks from the book's surface.
  • Too much at once. Most damage happens when someone tries several fixes in a row on the same card.

If a card is worth more to you than a few dollars, it is worth getting an opinion before trying anything.

What professional dent reduction can do

Professional dent reduction works with the card rather than against it: controlled moisture, gentle and even pressure, and plenty of time, with the card checked at every stage. The goal is to let the squashed fibres relax back toward their original shape without marking the surface or changing the card.

What to expect, honestly:

  • Reduced, not erased. Most dents can be made much less visible. Some disappear to the eye. Deep dents with broken fibres usually leave a trace.
  • Every card is different. Card stock, coating, holofoil and the age of the dent all change the result, which is why we look at photos first.
  • Time matters. Good results come from slow, controlled work, not a quick press.

Common questions

Can a dent in a Pokémon card be completely removed?

Sometimes it becomes invisible to the eye, but more often it is reduced rather than erased. Deep dents where the paper fibres have broken usually leave a trace. We tell you what is realistic from photos before any work starts.

Is it safe to fix a card dent at home?

Home methods such as steam, heat or heavy books can work on some cards, but they often cause water marks, coating damage or warping, especially in humid weather. If the card matters to you, get an opinion first.
