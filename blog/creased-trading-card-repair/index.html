Creases

Creased Trading Card? What Can and Can't Be Fixed

By Cardboard Clinic · 3 min read

A crease is the damage collectors dread most. The good news is that many creases can be softened a great deal. The honest news is that some parts of a crease cannot be undone. Knowing which is which helps you decide whether treatment is worth it.

Dent or crease?

A dent is a low spot where the paper has been squashed. A crease is a line where the card has been folded, even slightly, so the layers have been bent past the point where they spring back. Hold the card at an angle to a light. A dent looks like a soft dip. A crease looks like a line, often with a ridge on one side and a valley on the other.

The four kinds of crease

1. Light surface creases

A faint line you mostly see under a light, with the coating and ink intact. These usually respond well to treatment and can often be made very hard to see.

2. Hard creases

A clear fold line you can see straight on and feel with a finger. These can usually be flattened and softened a lot, but the line rarely vanishes completely, because the fibres along the fold have been broken.

3. Creases with whitening or cracked ink

When a fold is sharp enough, the ink and coating crack and the white paper underneath shows through as a thin line. Flattening the crease can make it much less obvious, but the missing ink does not grow back. We will not repaint or recolour a card to hide it.

4. Layer separation

Bad folds and corner damage can split the card's layers apart, so the edge looks thick or the corner feels soft. This needs specialist work and an honest conversation about the likely result before anything is done.

What crease repair actually does

Crease reduction uses the same principles as dent work: controlled moisture, careful, even pressure and patience. The aim is to relax the fold and bring the surface back to flat, so light no longer catches the line. It is slow work done in stages, with the card checked throughout.

  • What usually improves: the ridge and valley of the fold, how visible the line is at an angle, and how the card sits in a sleeve or toploader.
  • What usually stays: whitening where ink has cracked off, any colour loss, and a faint trace of a hard fold.

Is it worth fixing?

That depends on what the card means to you. Some collectors want a favourite card to look and sit right again. Others care mostly about grading, which brings in different questions. Read our guide to restoration and grading before you decide.

A simple rule: if the crease bothers you every time you look at the card, and the photos suggest it is the treatable kind, it is usually worth asking.

Before you do anything

  • Do not try to bend the card back the other way. That often doubles the damage.
  • Do not wet or iron the card.
  • Put it in a soft sleeve and a rigid toploader so the crease does not get worse.
  • Take photos of the front, the back, and the crease with light from the side, then send them to us on WhatsApp for a free first assessment.

Common questions

Can a creased Pokémon card be fixed?

Many creases can be softened a lot, and light surface creases can often be made hard to see. Hard creases usually leave a faint trace, and whitening where the ink has cracked cannot be reversed.

Can you fix whitening on a crease?

Flattening the crease can make whitening less obvious, but the missing ink does not come back. We do not repaint or recolour cards to hide it.

What is the difference between a dent and a crease?

A dent is a squashed low spot in the paper. A crease is a fold line where the layers have been bent past the point of springing back, often with a ridge and a valley.

How long does crease repair take?

It depends on the card and the crease. Treatment is done in stages with time to settle in between. Our queue page on the homepage shows current estimated waiting times.
